# Insights: Org Design

Atomic claims derived from CivilGrid Org Intelligence engagement and lab operating experience.

---

**Hierarchy is an information routing solution, not an organizational principle.**
Managers exist because humans have a span-of-control limit of 5–8. AI removes that constraint. The org model should be redesigned from first principles, not incrementally reformed.

**The minimum viable world model must be lightweight enough that a 50-person team will actually keep it current.**
The line between "enough structure to be useful" and "so much structure it's a burden" is the central design challenge. A neglected wiki decays faster than any technical failure.

**Series A (~50 people) is the last moment to choose the right organizational model. Every hire from here forward either reinforces a hierarchy or builds an intelligence.**
The wrong model gets baked in permanently. The window to design intentionally closes fast.

**Behavioral signal is honest; surveyed signal is not.**
Layer activation patterns, session return depth, and Coming Soon clicks reveal what users need. Feature request forms and satisfaction surveys reveal their theory of the product.

**An org intelligence layer must read behavioral signal, not just stated artifacts.**
A world model built only on what people write down reflects what they intend to do, not what they actually do. Both are required.

**The architecture must make reduced management feel like autonomy gained, not career path lost.**
Cultural resistance from people in or aspiring to coordination roles kills adoption before any technical failure. The framing is the design problem.
